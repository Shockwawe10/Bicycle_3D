I

Built in the order a mechanic would

  1. Frame

    On the stand first. Every other part is placed from it: the head tube, the dropouts, the bottom bracket.

  2. Fork

    Slides up into the head tube from below, so the front end can take a wheel.

  3. Wheels

    Rolled in along the ground, the front from ahead and the rear from behind, its cassette already on.

  4. Drivetrain

    The cranks come in through the bottom bracket, the far arm from its own side, pedalling forward as they land. The chain goes on last, once there is a ring and a cog to wrap.

  5. Cockpit

    Lowered onto the steerer, turning into line as it drops.

  6. Saddle

    Last: the post slides into the seat tube until the saddle sits 725 mm from the bottom bracket. The bike is whole.

II

In detail

Every part is modelled, down to the bolts. Choose a point on the bike to look closer.

Frame and fork

Carbon, aero-profiled. The down tube deepens from 49 to 62 mm into the bottom bracket, the chainstays are 410 mm on every size, and the fork has 44.4 mm of offset. The geometry for your size is in the next chapter.

Drivetrain

52/36 chainrings on a four-arm spider and 170 mm cranks, into an 11–30 cassette. The chain is solved link by link, 112 links with every roller seated, and runs here on the 52 and the 15, over two 11-tooth jockey wheels.

Brakes

Flat-mount calipers on the side away from the chain, on rotors of 160 mm up front and 140 mm at the rear, each drilled with 30 holes on a six-arm carrier. The rear wheel runs on a 142 mm thru-axle through slotted dropouts.

Wheels

Rims with a hooked bead seat and a rounded spoke bed, on 26 mm tyres with a file tread on the shoulders. 24 bladed spokes a wheel: radial at the front, two-cross at the rear. The front turns on a 100 mm thru-axle.

Cockpit

The stem runs 100 mm at −6° into a 420 mm bar with flattened tops. The hoods sit on the forward bend, and the tape wraps the drops.
