# Varsen: brief

**Interviewed 2026-11-12.** Answers are quoted as the owner gave them (voice input, kept verbatim).
Where the owner delegated a decision ("propose the idea"), the decision is marked **authored** and
the choice they then made is recorded.

## What this is

A redo of the Varsen landing page. Varsen is a concept carbon road-bike brand and a web portfolio
piece. Its asset is a procedural 3D bike built in the browser from a published geometry chart
(Specialized Tarmac SL7, 2021/22). **The bike model is kept as built.** Everything around it is
redone: structure, identity, motion, ending.

> "So I'm looking to use this skill to redo my website. I'm looking for this one to be premium,
> trusted. I'm looking for this one to be more editorial rather than a bunch of different AI-generated
> assets and not like a world that we'd be flying through. I'm looking for this one to be more premium."
>
> "Highlights: Premium and modern UI/UX design. Smooth scroll animations and transitions. Responsive
> website layout. Product-focused storytelling. Cinematic visual experience."
>
> "Keep the model of the bike"

The five highlights are acceptance criteria, checked in the verification pass.

## The eight topics

1. **Vibe and references.** Premium, trusted, editorial, modern, cinematic.
   References: "First, what should feel like it belonged to something premium". No named references.
2. **The journey.** "Second, should be screen the bike itself, product first, and cinematic."
   The rest of the sequence is **authored** (Journey, below).
3. **Energy.** "Energy, it should be feel something mid between the calm and intense"
4. **Feeling and the one moment.** "Fourth. I agree." This answers the question of whether the
   part-by-part build survives as a chapter: it does. The curve is **authored** to the owner's
   choices (Feeling curve, below). As first built, the peak was the signature move they picked; after
   the owner's second review it moved to chapter III (see Revisions).
5. **Something no other site does.** "Fifth, I don't have proposed the idea". Three were proposed.
   Chosen: **"Rebuild at your size"**: pick a frame size, or give your height, and the real 3D bike
   rebuilds itself at that size from the published chart.
6. **How far from premium-minimal.** Premium and editorial, from the opening message.
7. **One world or distinct scenes.** "not like a world that we'd be flying through". Distinct
   chapters. No worldflight.
8. **Assets.** The bike model only. "rather than a bunch of different AI-generated assets": **no
   generated imagery at all.** Every image on the page is a render of the real model.
   Identity: "replace for the logo a logo, brand colors, and fonts". Look: "Yellow and black like
   lamborghini using" (the message was cut off after "using"). The identity is original: no
   Lamborghini name, bull, shield or typeface. Yellow and black, the bike repainted gloss yellow.

**Ending.** "Sixth, by the end, replace with some replace with something real trusted". Chosen:
**"Calendly booking"**. The visitor's chosen size is carried into the booking. The page must never
show a booking it cannot actually make. 2026-11-14, the owner supplied the event: "https://calendly.com/johntraycey/30min
add link to the book a fitting". 2026-11-14, then: "just do an external page open". The button is a plain
link that opens the calendar in a new tab, the chosen size added to it as a1.

## Honesty rules (from "trusted")

- Real numbers only, each with its source. Geometry for all seven sizes: Bike Insights' listing of
  the Specialized Tarmac SL7 frameset, model year 2022.
- Rider height to size: a retailer's reference chart (Bike Mart), which the page labels as a
  reference and not the maker's. Between ranges, the page shows both sizes and says a fitting decides.
- Saddle height from inseam: LeMond's method, inseam × 0.883, labelled as a starting point.
- What is not size-specific in this model is said so: cockpit (100 mm stem, 420 mm bar) and 170 mm
  cranks are shown as on the size 56.
- Varsen is a concept. The colophon says so. No testimonials, prices, dates, stock or counters of
  anything invented.

## Revisions after the first review (owner's words, verbatim)

**2026-11-12.** On the dimension lines: "I don't like the measurements in a designer way. Because it's
feels a little bit cheap". On the drivetrain: "there is something wrong about the color of the chain.
Because it's while it's turning, it's going dark, and there's also true for the breaks. So maybe
lighten them up". Done: the dimensions became a technical drawing (hairlines, ticks, dashed
extensions, nodes on the measured points, stack set upright off the bike); the chain and rotors got
their own satin metal that the key light reads on from every side.

**2026-11-13.** "I don't really like the section of how does it called? Up close. ... I don't like the
pictures inside that. Maybe we should change it entirely. To maybe some kind of hero block or product
experience block." And: "I don't like another section when we go through the scrolling and the model
size kind of change. ... we should remove it entirely."

Done: the Up close stills and the Seven sizes sweep are gone. Chapter III is now **In detail**, a
product-experience block on one pinned stage: it opens as a hero frame, then the camera makes one
orbit of the live bike, holding on each part while its caption is read. The size chooser (Find
yours) stays and becomes chapter IV; the bike still rebuilds itself at the visitor's size there, but
nothing changes size under the scroll any more. The journey, curve and peak below are re-authored to
fit (the owner directed the change; the new shape is **authored**).

**2026-11-13, later.** "There is also be good to add like an arrow that shows people so they need to scroll it
down to see how the bicycle creates actually. So it's not very intuitive for the user." Done, against the skill's
default (it bans scroll cues: "they are looking at the hero; they know"): the owner has seen visitors miss that
scrolling builds the bike. The cue says what scrolling does ("Scroll to see it built"), sits beside the hero's action
as a quieter second link, fades once the visitor scrolls, and links to the build; the build's caption adds "It builds
as you scroll".

Then: "the scroll also needs to be kind of presented on the build. Maybe the smaller ... Not something big." Done: a
smaller "Keep scrolling" with the same arrow at the bottom right of the assembly plate, opposite its caption, visible
while parts are still to come and gone as the saddle lands. Phones say "Scroll", and the caption drops its last
sentence there so the two never collide.

Then, on the chart chapter: "I think that we don't need this section anymore, because later we have the exact same
measurements ... maybe replace it with something else ... maybe some features abilities or something like that."
Chosen from three proposals: **an at-a-glance band**. The chart chapter (side view, technical drawing, geometry table)
is gone; its drawing and table live on in Find yours. In its place, between the frontispiece and the build, a short band
of four real figures with one line each (7 sizes; 45 mm carbon rims; 2 x 12 speeds; a one-piece cockpit), every one the
model's own. The trust line ("built from those numbers, measured back against them") and its source moved into Find
yours, beside the table it is about. The chapters renumber: I Build, II In detail, III Find yours, IV Book.

Then, on In detail: "I don't want to details appear while scrolling. I wanted to be like points on the bike on which
user can click, and it zooms out and tells exactly about which part of the bike it is." Done: the scroll tour (pin 4.4)
is gone. In detail is one screen: the size 56 in three-quarter view with five points on it (Frame, Drivetrain, Brakes,
Wheels, Cockpit). Choosing one flies the camera in to that part (read as "zooms in on it") while the column beside it
says what it is, every fact the model's own; Back, Previous / Next, the arrow keys and Escape move between them. The
visitor's own choice now drives the peak, not the scroll.

Then: "we have the duplication of the text on the first section when the vehicle is assembling, and on the second when
it's tells much in details ... maybe point to the other things, or not point, just change the text." Done: the points stay,
and each fact now lives in one section. At a glance: the four headline figures with plain words, no other numbers. The
build: the assembly story (what goes on, in what order and why, how it moves), with the saddle height its one number.
In detail: the specs and what only shows up close (four-arm spider, 11-tooth jockey wheels, 30-hole rotors on six-arm
carriers, radial front and two-cross rear lacing, file-tread tyres, flattened bar tops), every figure read from the
model. The chart geometry stays in Find yours alone.

Then: "the background of this scene, we have like 56 ... I want to add the brand model like R1. And it should be stated
across the side that is specifically the new, the most highest reliable model, etc." Done: the figure reads "R1", and a
line reads up the right edge: "New · Varsen R1 · The flagship road bike". "The most reliable" was left out on purpose: the
brief is "trusted" and Varsen has no reliability data, so an unbacked superlative is the one claim it cannot make. It
can be added with a source if the owner has one.

Then: "the title ... should be more visible. It's right now it's not really visible and not really there. So maybe you
read the skill taste and place it whenever it feels right." Done per the taste skill (nothing tilted; the first screen
says what is sold): the line moved from the margin to directly above the headline, as a solid yellow NEW tag and the
model line in the display face.

## Journey (authored, revised 2026-11-13)

```
Frontispiece  Awe           the finished bike, yellow on black, lit like a car reveal; you can turn it
Glance        Orientation   four real figures, one line each: what this bike is, at a glance
I    Build    Fascination   it builds itself in the order a mechanic would
II   Detail   Curiosity     points on the bike; choose one and the camera flies in   <- PEAK
III  Yours    Ownership     give your height and it rebuilds itself as yours; every number sourced
Close         Resolve       book the fitting, your size already filled in
```

## Feeling curve (authored, revised 2026-11-13)

| Act | Feeling | What on screen causes it |
|---|---|---|
| Frontispiece | Awe | A yellow bike alone in a dark studio. The key light comes up across its clearcoat and it turns under your hand. |
| Glance | Orientation | Four large figures arrive one after another, each with a one-line fact. Quick, quiet, no picture. |
| I Build | Fascination | The frame waits; fork, wheels, drivetrain, cockpit, saddle arrive in workshop order, nothing passing through anything. |
| II In detail | Curiosity, then reward | The bike holds still with five pulsing points on it. You choose one and the camera flies in close: the chain link by link, round the back to the brake rotor, up to the cockpit. You go back, or on to the next. |
| III Yours | Ownership and trust | You give your height; the bike rebuilds itself at your size and every number on the sheet changes. Each number is the published chart, and the page says so beside them. |
| Close | Resolve | One calendar, your size already in it. The page stops. |

No two adjacent rows share a feeling. Energy sits in the middle: calm acts (Glance, Yours) sit between
the moving ones (Frontispiece, Build, In detail). Build and In detail are neighbours, so In detail
holds still until the visitor chooses: the quiet the peak arrives from is the visitor's own pause.

## The peak

> "I clicked the chain and the camera flew in close enough to count the links; then I went round to the brakes."

It lives in II, and it is the visitor's: a deliberate exception to the skill's rule that the peak gets the most scroll
room. An interactive peak is paced by the visitor's choices, not by scroll distance (the owner asked for points to
click, 2026-11-13), so II is one screen and the build (3.5 to 3.9) is now the longest act. The peak keeps the page's
only camera moves that leave the bike's own side, and the stillness in front of them.

## Tell-someone sentence

> It's the site where you click a part of the bike and the camera flies in close enough to count the
> chain links, and then the bike rebuilds itself at your height.

The signature move (rebuild at your size) and the peak are two moments; the sentence carries both, in the order the
visitor meets them.

## Authored silence

- **II** holds still until the visitor chooses a point: nothing in it is driven by scroll.
